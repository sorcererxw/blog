# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site serves people who want a fast read on sorcererxw's public work: engineers, founders, potential collaborators, recruiters, and long-term readers. They arrive with limited context and should quickly understand the author's technical taste, writing depth, shipped projects, and public notes without navigating a traditional blog archive.

## Product Purpose

This is a public Personal Site, not a blog index. The homepage introduces one authored identity first, then presents a unified overview feed of writing, projects, and social posts. Success means a visitor can understand the person, browse the mixed body of work, and follow durable article detail links from one coherent public surface.

## Brand Personality

The brand should feel exacting, technically literate, and authored. It can be opinionated and distinctive, but it should not feel loud for its own sake. The interface should carry confidence through structure, typography, evidence, and restrained interaction rather than through generic decoration.

## Anti-references

Do not make the site feel like a template blog archive, a SaaS landing page, an admin dashboard, or a portfolio full of identical project cards. Avoid generic AI-page signals: gradient text, glassy panels, decorative blobs, repeated icon-card grids, oversized hero metrics, and vague "modern personal brand" copy. `/blog` must not return as a primary product surface; writing belongs in the homepage overview feed and article detail pages.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with identity, then evidence. The Profile Hero should establish the author before the feed asks visitors to browse.
2. Keep one public surface coherent. Writing, projects, and social posts should feel like one authored stream, not three unrelated sections.
3. Preserve source truth without exposing plumbing. Notion, Telegram, Cloudflare, and routing details should support the experience without becoming the experience.
4. Make density readable. The feed can be information-rich, but hierarchy, spacing, and media treatment must keep scanning comfortable.
5. Prefer distinctive restraint. The design should have a point of view while avoiding both bland minimalism and decorative excess.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG AA as the baseline for contrast, focus visibility, keyboard navigation, and semantic structure. Support reduced-motion preferences. Do not communicate state by color alone. Keep responsive layouts free of overlap, horizontal overflow, and text clipping on mobile and desktop.
